This study investigates how cloud cover affects the ability of optical satellite imagery to accurately capture river
conditions globally. Using 22 years of daily cloud cover data and modeled river discharge for over 21,000 river reaches
worldwide, we show significant biases in satellite observations, particularly in tropical and Arctic regions. The
findings demonstrate that cloud-related biases are geographically organized, potentially impacting large-scale
comparisons of river conditions derived from satellite data. By quantifying these biases and exploring their
relationship with various environmental factors, this work provides insights for improving the interpretation and
application of satellite-based river observations in hydrological studies.
